## Why age

M001 uses `age_band` directly as a model variable (derived in the SAS data step from `age`: 1 = under 30,
2 = 30-44, 3 = 45 and over; band 3 is the reference level). Age is a protected characteristic under the UK
Equality Act 2010. For financial services, Schedule 3 paragraph 20A (inserted by the Equality Act 2010 (Age
Exceptions) Order 2012) permits age-based differences, provided that where a risk assessment considers age, it
uses information that is relevant to the risk and comes from a source on which it is reasonable to rely. So the
question for M001 is not whether age may be used at all, but whether its use is justified by evidence, whether it
produces outcomes we can defend, and how it is monitored. The FCA's Consumer Duty expectations on fair outcomes
also apply to UK lenders.

## Method

- Score: the legacy PD (`pd_legacy`); higher = riskier. An applicant is *declined* when the score is at or above
  a cut-off chosen to decline as many applicants as the observed bad rate (the decision rule is illustrative; the
  bank's real cut-off should replace it).
- Metrics (`mrf.validation.fairness`): approval rate per band and the **adverse impact ratio** against the band with
  the highest approval rate (the four-fifths rule flags ratios below 0.80); **TPR** = share of bads declined and
  **FPR** = share of goods declined per band (equal opportunity / equalised odds); AUC within each band.
- Thresholds for the report's traffic light: `config/pipeline.yaml` `reporting.disparate_impact_green` /
  `disparate_impact_amber`.

## Reading the results (draft)

1. **Approval gap.** Applicants under 30 are approved markedly less often than applicants aged 45 and over; on the
   fixture the ratio is below the four-fifths line, so the analysis report shows fairness as red.
2. **Part of the gap is risk.** The observed bad rate is also much higher in the youngest band. Approval parity
   alone therefore does not prove unfair treatment: a model that tracks real risk will approve fewer applicants in
   a riskier group.
3. **The more telling signal is among good payers.** Under-30 applicants who repaid are declined more often than
   good payers aged 45 and over (higher FPR). That is the outcome a customer would experience as unfair, and it is
   what an equal-opportunity view measures.
4. **Small sample.** Band 1 has few records in the fixture, so every rate has wide uncertainty. Conclusions need
   the full out-of-time sample (and ideally confidence intervals) before sign-off.

## Mitigation options (A3 to choose and justify)

| Option | What it does | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Justify and monitor | Keep `age_band`, document the evidence that age is predictive (relevant and reliable data, per para 20A), add age-band approval rate and FPR to monitoring with an amber/red threshold | No outcome change; relies on the justification holding up |
| B. Refit without `age_band` | Remove the variable and refit; compare Gini and the band metrics | Usually small Gini loss; age can still enter through correlated variables (e.g. duration, credit history), so re-measure |
| C. Manual review band | Route declined applicants close to the cut-off (in every band) to an underwriter | Operational cost; reduces wrongful declines of good payers |
| D. Constrained / post-processed model | Reweigh training data or adjust scores to narrow the FPR gap | Explicit trade-off between accuracy and parity; needs governance and legal sign-off |
| E. Band-specific cut-offs | Different cut-offs per age band | Not recommended: direct differential treatment, hard to justify |

**Suggested default for the demo (A3 to confirm):** option A now (documented justification + monitoring), and
option B as a challenger model in the validation report, with the decision recorded at H2.
